# Brief Format Reference

This is the format standard the Strategist aims for. The prompt already enforces it. Keep this file as a reference for judging whether a brief is good, and for writing your own briefs by hand if you ever want to.

## Default brief structure

1. `*Top 3 Today*`
   Terse bullets only. One line per item. Name the person, project, or number and state the action.

2. `*Follow-Through*`
   Compare today's situation with the previous briefs' Top 3 lists. Every item from the most recent prior Top 3 gets one status: `✅ moved/done`, `⏳ still open`, or `❓ unclear from the notes`. An item present across three or more briefs must be flagged `🔁 CARRIED <n> BRIEFS`, then either become #1 with a smaller next step or be explicitly killed or parked with a reason. End with exactly one hard focus question.

3. `*Risk Radar*` and `*Money Radar*`
   Hard deadlines, stale relationships, and concrete money items only. Keep it short. "Clear" is a valid answer.

4. `*Mission Check*`
   One sharp, specific line grounded in the actual state of the business. Cite carry-over evidence from Follow-Through, not generic advice.

5. `*Council:*`
   One concise chairman summary line with a score out of 10 and any dissent.

## Weekly addition

Weekly reviews include `*Follow-Through Scorecard*` immediately after `*What Moved*`. State how many of last week's Top 3 items actually moved as a fraction, then name the evidence behind the count.

## Rules

- Do not add a preamble before the first section
- Do not restate the date or the report title in the body
- Insert one blank line between each section so the brief is easy to scan
- Keep bullets terse and non-redundant
- If a person, issue, or task appears twice, keep it only in the most relevant section
- Separate facts from inference
- If inputs are stale or missing, say so plainly rather than pretending certainty
- Prefer one strong insight over three weak ones
- Briefs are saved automatically to the briefs folder with the date in the filename

## Bad brief patterns to avoid

- Vague reassurance without evidence
- Long narrative with no decision point
- Dumping raw inputs back out with no synthesis
- Repeating the same issue in multiple sections
- Reporting activity instead of progress
- Hiding uncertainty or stale inputs

## Example

```text
*Top 3 Today*
- Send the referral page to your 12 past clients: drafted 9 days ago, zero revenue until it ships.
- Chase invoice 042 (3 weeks unpaid): one polite nudge email, today.
- Confirm scope with Jordan before Thursday's kickoff: prevents a free scope creep week.

*Follow-Through*
- Referral page: ⏳ still open. 🔁 CARRIED 3 BRIEFS, make "send to the first three clients" today's #1 step.
- Invoice 042: ✅ moved/done, payment promised Friday.
- Jordan scope: ❓ unclear from the notes.
- What are you willing to stop polishing so one revenue-moving action ships today?

*Risk Radar*
- Jordan has not replied in 6 days and kickoff is Thursday.

*Money Radar*
- Invoice 042 unpaid 3 weeks. Software renewals due next month: cancel the two unused tools first.

*Stuck On You vs Stuck On Others*
- You: referral page send, invoice nudge.
- Others: Jordan's scope reply (oldest item, nudge today).

*Mission Check*
The podcast research is drift: the referral page has carried across three briefs while no send has happened.

*Council:* SHIP (7.5/10). Dissent, Munger: the fastest way to fail the quarter is another week of polishing instead of sending.
```
